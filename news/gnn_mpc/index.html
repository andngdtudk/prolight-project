Overview

This page accompanies the paper Learned Network Dynamics for Model Predictive Traffic Signal Control under Incidents (D. V. A. Nguyen, C. Lima Azevedo, T. Toledo and F. Rodrigues). GNN-MPC plans signal phases with an action-conditioned, two-level graph model of network traffic dynamics that incorporates queue measurements upstream of short controlled lanes, and coordinates the intersections through iterated best responses.

The paper and its Supplementary Material report the pooled results, the definitions of all metrics and controllers, and the controlled comparisons. This page collects the material that complements them: per-scenario tables, secondary metrics, the full prediction errors of the learned model and the training curves of the reinforcement-learning baselines. Tables and figures are numbered W1, W2, … to distinguish them from those of the paper.

The individual experiment runs on both networks, including the time series of each run, can be explored in the ProLight experiment dashboard. The simulation environment is T-REX.

Reading the tables

Network ATT is the mean travel time of the vehicles admitted into the network, measured from their insertion. Traveller ATT is the mean over all scheduled vehicles, measured from the scheduled departure, so that it includes the delay before insertion and charges a vehicle that is never inserted the remaining evaluation window. Throughput counts the trips completed within the window, and the network AWT is the mean time the admitted vehicles spend below 0.1 m/s. On the grid, every vehicle is admitted under every controller, so the two travel times coincide within 0.2 s.

Paired differences are computed between runs that share the scenario, seed, demand and incidents, and are given as mean ± half-width of the 95% Student-t interval. Unless stated otherwise, they are GNN-MPC minus the other controller, so a negative travel or waiting time and a positive throughput favor GNN-MPC. RL baselines are labelled with their training condition: (NI) without and (I) with incidents.

Gate behavior

The gate γ of the network-level mixture of experts is trained through the prediction loss alone, without incident labels (Section IV of the main paper), so whether it separates the regimes is an empirical question; Figure W5 shows that it does. The one-step γ has a median of 0.0002 (grid) and 0.0007 (Ingolstadt) on normal intersections and of 0.9995 and 1.000 on incident-affected ones, exceeding 0.5 in 5% and 12% of the normal samples against 63% and 90% of the incident-affected ones (Figure W5a, d). The gate does not copy the incident flag: aligned to onset, the median γ rises from near zero over 200–300 s, consistent with the gradual build-up of the queue behind the incident (Figure W5b, e), whereas at clearance it falls from 0.99 to about zero within two to four steps (Figure W5c, f). Not every incident engages the incident expert: γ stays below 0.5 throughout 14 of the 38 grid test incidents, all of them long, and 1 of 18 on Ingolstadt; which expert serves an incident is decided by the prediction loss, and the experts' names describe what they predominantly handle rather than a constraint imposed in training.
